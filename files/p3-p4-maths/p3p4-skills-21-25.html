Skill 21

Integrating Partial Fractions

What It Is

When you need to integrate a rational function (a polynomial divided by a polynomial), you often cannot integrate it directly. The trick is to first split the fraction into simpler pieces called partial fractions, then integrate each piece separately using the standard logarithm rule.

Each partial fraction has the form \(\dfrac{A}{ax+b}\), which integrates neatly to a logarithm. This converts one hard integral into a sum of easy integrals.

This technique only applies to proper fractions where the top has a lower degree than the bottom. If the fraction is improper, you must do algebraic division first (see Skill 25).

The Rule

Standard logarithm integral:

$$\int \frac{A}{ax+b}\,dx = \frac{A}{a}\ln|ax+b| + c$$

For a repeated linear factor:

$$\int \frac{A}{(ax+b)^2}\,dx = \frac{-A}{a(ax+b)} + c$$

General approach: Split into partial fractions, then apply the rule above to each term separately.

Where to Apply It

Use this when you are asked to integrate a fraction whose denominator factorises into linear factors. Common exam triggers: "find \(\int \dfrac{f(x)}{(x+a)(x+b)}\,dx\)" or definite integral versions of the same. Always check the fraction is proper first.

Worked Example 1

Find \(\displaystyle\int \frac{5x+1}{(x+1)(2x-1)}\,dx\).

Step 1 — Set up partial fractions

Write \(\dfrac{5x+1}{(x+1)(2x-1)} \equiv \dfrac{A}{x+1} + \dfrac{B}{2x-1}\).

Step 2 — Multiply both sides by the denominator
$$5x+1 \equiv A(2x-1) + B(x+1)$$
Step 3 — Substitute roots to find A and B

Let \(x = -1\): \(\;5(-1)+1 = A(2(-1)-1)\;\Rightarrow\; -4 = -3A \;\Rightarrow\; A = \dfrac{4}{3}\).

Let \(x = \dfrac{1}{2}\): \(\;5\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)+1 = B\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+1\right) \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{7}{2} = \dfrac{3B}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; B = \dfrac{7}{3}\).

Step 4 — Rewrite and integrate
$$\int \frac{5x+1}{(x+1)(2x-1)}\,dx = \int \frac{\frac{4}{3}}{x+1}\,dx + \int \frac{\frac{7}{3}}{2x-1}\,dx$$ $$= \frac{4}{3}\ln|x+1| + \frac{7}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\ln|2x-1| + c$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{4}{3}\ln|x+1| + \frac{7}{6}\ln|2x-1| + c}$$
Worked Example 2

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{1} \frac{3}{(x+1)(x+2)}\,dx\).

Step 1 — Partial fractions
$$\frac{3}{(x+1)(x+2)} \equiv \frac{A}{x+1}+\frac{B}{x+2}$$

Multiply through: \(3 \equiv A(x+2)+B(x+1)\).

Step 2 — Find constants

Let \(x=-1\): \(3 = A(1)\Rightarrow A=3\).

Let \(x=-2\): \(3 = B(-1)\Rightarrow B=-3\).

Step 3 — Integrate
$$\int_0^1 \frac{3}{x+1} - \frac{3}{x+2}\,dx = \Big[3\ln|x+1| - 3\ln|x+2|\Big]_0^1$$ $$= \Big[3\ln\!\left(\frac{x+1}{x+2}\right)\Big]_0^1$$
Step 4 — Apply limits
$$= 3\ln\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) - 3\ln\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = 3\ln\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\times 2\right) = 3\ln\!\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{3\ln\!\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)}$$

Find \(\displaystyle\int \frac{7x-1}{(x-3)(2x+1)}\,dx\).

Question 7

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_1^2 \frac{8x}{(x^2-1)(x+1)}\,dx\). [Hint: \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\).]

Full Solutions
Solution 1

\(\dfrac{4}{(x+1)(x-1)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x-1}\). Multiply: \(4\equiv A(x-1)+B(x+1)\).

\(x=1:\;4=2B\Rightarrow B=2.\quad x=-1:\;4=-2A\Rightarrow A=-2.\)

$$\int \frac{-2}{x+1}+\frac{2}{x-1}\,dx = \boxed{-2\ln|x+1|+2\ln|x-1|+c}$$
Solution 2

\(\dfrac{7x-1}{(x-3)(2x+1)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-3}+\dfrac{B}{2x+1}\). Multiply: \(7x-1\equiv A(2x+1)+B(x-3)\).

\(x=3:\;20=7A\Rightarrow A=\dfrac{20}{7}.\quad x=-\tfrac{1}{2}:\;-\tfrac{9}{2}=-\tfrac{7B}{2}\Rightarrow B=\dfrac{9}{7}.\)

$$\boxed{\frac{20}{7}\ln|x-3|+\frac{9}{14}\ln|2x+1|+c}$$
Solution 3

\(\dfrac{6}{(x-1)(x+2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}.\quad x=1:\;6=3A\Rightarrow A=2.\quad x=-2:\;6=-3B\Rightarrow B=-2.\)

$$\Big[2\ln|x-1|-2\ln|x+2|\Big]_2^4 = \left(2\ln 3-2\ln 6\right)-\left(2\ln 1-2\ln 4\right)$$ $$= 2\ln 3-2\ln 6-0+2\ln 4 = 2\ln\!\frac{12}{6} = \boxed{2\ln 2}$$
Solution 4

\(\dfrac{5}{x(x+5)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x}+\dfrac{B}{x+5}.\quad x=0:\;5=5A\Rightarrow A=1.\quad x=-5:\;5=-5B\Rightarrow B=-1.\)

$$\boxed{\ln|x|-\ln|x+5|+c \;=\;\ln\!\left|\frac{x}{x+5}\right|+c}$$
Solution 5

\(\dfrac{3x+11}{(x+1)(x+3)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x+3}.\quad x=-1:\;8=2A\Rightarrow A=4.\quad x=-3:\;2=-2B\Rightarrow B=-1.\)

$$\boxed{4\ln|x+1|-\ln|x+3|+c}$$
Solution 6

\(\dfrac{1}{(2x+1)(x-2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{2x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x-2}.\quad x=2:\;1=5B\Rightarrow B=\tfrac{1}{5}.\quad x=-\tfrac{1}{2}:\;1=-\tfrac{5A}{2}\Rightarrow A=-\tfrac{2}{5}.\)

$$\int\frac{-\frac{2}{5}}{2x+1}+\frac{\frac{1}{5}}{x-2}\,dx = \boxed{-\frac{1}{5}\ln|2x+1|+\frac{1}{5}\ln|x-2|+c}$$
Solution 7

The denominator is \((x-1)(x+1)^2\). Split: \(\dfrac{8x}{(x-1)(x+1)^2}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+1}+\dfrac{C}{(x+1)^2}\).

Multiply through: \(8x\equiv A(x+1)^2+B(x-1)(x+1)+C(x-1)\).

\(x=1:\;8=4A\Rightarrow A=2.\quad x=-1:\;-8=-2C\Rightarrow C=4.\)

Coefficient of \(x^2:\;0=A+B\Rightarrow B=-2.\)

$$\Big[2\ln|x-1|-2\ln|x+1|-\frac{4}{x+1}\Big]_1^2$$

At \(x=2\): \(2\ln 1-2\ln 3-\tfrac{4}{3}=0-2\ln 3-\tfrac{4}{3}\).

At \(x=1\): \(2\ln 0-\cdots\) — note the lower limit \(x=1\) makes \(\ln|x-1|\) diverge. For this course the integral is improper; re-examining: \(\int_1^2\) with \(x=1\) being the root of \((x-1)\). The answer left in terms of the antiderivative evaluated at limit \(x\to1^+\) gives \(+\infty\), so the integral diverges. In exam contexts this question would typically avoid such a singularity. With the original factoring \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\), we have denominator \((x^2-1)(x+1)=(x-1)(x+1)^2\) and the lower limit \(x=1\) is a singularity. The correct answer is: \(\boxed{\text{The integral diverges (improper integral with singularity at }x=1\text{)}}\).

Skill 22

Integrating Trigonometric Functions Using Identities

What It Is

You cannot directly integrate expressions like \(\sin^2 x\), \(\cos^2 x\), \(\sin x\cos x\), or \(\tan^2 x\) in their current form. The strategy is to replace them with equivalent identities that turn the expression into something you already know how to integrate.

The key toolkit is the double-angle and Pythagorean identities. Once you rewrite the integrand, every term becomes a standard integral (sin, cos, sec², or a constant) that you can handle directly.

This is essentially an algebraic manipulation step before you integrate, not a new integration rule. You transform the problem, then use known results.

Key Identities for Integration

Powers of sine and cosine — use double angle:

$$\cos 2x = 1 - 2\sin^2 x \;\Rightarrow\; \sin^2 x = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$$ $$\cos 2x = 2\cos^2 x - 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \cos^2 x = \frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}$$

Product of sine and cosine:

$$\sin x\cos x = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x$$

Tangent squared — use Pythagorean identity:

$$\tan^2 x = \sec^2 x - 1$$

Standard results needed after substitution:

$$\int\cos kx\,dx = \frac{\sin kx}{k}+c,\quad \int\sin kx\,dx = -\frac{\cos kx}{k}+c,\quad \int\sec^2 x\,dx = \tan x+c$$
Where to Apply It

Use whenever the integrand contains: \(\sin^2 x\), \(\cos^2 x\), \(\tan^2 x\), \(\sin x\cos x\), \(\sin^2 x\cos^2 x\), or any product/power of trig functions that does not immediately reduce to a standard form. The signal in the exam is: "the integral looks impossible" — that means reach for an identity.

Worked Example 1

Find \(\displaystyle\int 3\sin^2 x\,dx\).

Step 1 — Replace \(\sin^2 x\) using the double angle identity
$$\sin^2 x = \frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}$$

So the integral becomes:

$$\int 3\cdot\frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}\,dx = \int \frac{3}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\cos 2x\,dx$$
Step 2 — Integrate term by term
$$= \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{\sin 2x}{2} + c$$
Step 3 — Simplify
$$= \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{3}{4}\sin 2x + c$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{3}{2}x - \frac{3}{4}\sin 2x + c}$$
Worked Example 2

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}\!\left(\tan^2 x + \sin x\cos x\right)dx\).

Step 1 — Replace each term

\(\tan^2 x = \sec^2 x - 1\) and \(\sin x\cos x = \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x\).

$$\int_0^{\pi/4}\!\left(\sec^2 x - 1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x\right)dx$$
Step 2 — Integrate
$$= \Big[\tan x - x - \frac{1}{4}\cos 2x\Big]_0^{\pi/4}$$
Step 3 — Apply limits

At \(x=\dfrac{\pi}{4}\): \(\tan\dfrac{\pi}{4} - \dfrac{\pi}{4} - \dfrac{1}{4}\cos\dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1 - \dfrac{\pi}{4} - 0 = 1-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\).

At \(x=0\): \(\tan 0 - 0 - \dfrac{1}{4}\cos 0 = 0-0-\dfrac{1}{4} = -\dfrac{1}{4}\).

Step 4 — Subtract
$$\left(1-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)-\left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = 1-\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{1}{4} = \frac{5}{4}-\frac{\pi}{4}$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\pi}{4}}$$

Full Solutions

Use \(\cos^2 x = \dfrac{1+\cos 2x}{2}\).

$$\int\frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}\,dx = \boxed{\frac{x}{2}+\frac{\sin 2x}{4}+c}$$
Solution 2

Use \(\sin^2 3x = \dfrac{1-\cos 6x}{2}\).

$$\int 4\cdot\frac{1-\cos 6x}{2}\,dx = \int 2-2\cos 6x\,dx = \boxed{2x - \frac{\sin 6x}{3}+c}$$
Solution 3

Use \(\tan^2 x = \sec^2 x-1\).

$$\int(\sec^2 x-1)\,dx = \boxed{\tan x - x + c}$$
Solution 4

$$\left[\frac{x}{2}-\frac{\sin 2x}{4}\right]_0^{\pi/2} = \left(\frac{\pi}{4}-0\right)-(0-0) = \boxed{\frac{\pi}{4}}$$

Use \(\sin x\cos x = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin 2x\), so \(6\sin x\cos x = 3\sin 2x\).

$$\int 3\sin 2x\,dx = \boxed{-\frac{3}{2}\cos 2x + c}$$
Solution 6

Use \(\cos^2 2x = \dfrac{1+\cos 4x}{2}\).

$$\int\frac{1+\cos 4x}{2}\,dx = \boxed{\frac{x}{2}+\frac{\sin 4x}{8}+c}$$
Solution 7

\(1+\tan^2 x = \sec^2 x\), so the integral is \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^2 x\,dx\).

$$\Big[\tan x\Big]_0^{\pi/4} = \tan\frac{\pi}{4}-\tan 0 = 1-0 = \boxed{1}$$
Skill 23

Partial Fractions (Distinct, Repeated & Improper)

What It Is

Partial fractions is the process of splitting a single complicated fraction into a sum of simpler fractions. Think of it as the reverse of adding fractions. You start with the result and work backwards to find what simpler fractions add up to give it.

The denominator of the original fraction determines the form of the partial fractions. Distinct (different) linear factors give one term each. A repeated linear factor \((ax+b)^2\) requires two terms. An improper fraction (top degree ≥ bottom degree) requires a polynomial quotient term as well.

Partial fractions are used heavily in both integration (Skill 21) and binomial expansion (Skill 24). Mastering the decomposition is essential before tackling those applications.

The Three Forms

Form 1 — Distinct linear factors:

$$\frac{f(x)}{(ax+b)(cx+d)} \equiv \frac{A}{ax+b}+\frac{B}{cx+d}$$

Form 2 — Repeated linear factor:

$$\frac{f(x)}{(ax+b)^2(cx+d)} \equiv \frac{A}{ax+b}+\frac{B}{(ax+b)^2}+\frac{C}{cx+d}$$

Form 3 — Improper fraction (degree of numerator ≥ degree of denominator):

First perform algebraic long division to write it as \(\text{polynomial} + \text{proper fraction}\), then decompose the proper fraction part.

$$\frac{\text{cubic}}{\text{quadratic}} \equiv (px+q) + \frac{rx+s}{(ax+b)(cx+d)}$$
Where to Apply It

Use partial fractions whenever you need to simplify a rational expression before integrating, expanding using the binomial series, or solving a differential equation. Check whether the fraction is proper first. If not, divide first (Skill 25), then decompose the remainder.

Worked Example 1 — Repeated Factor

Express \(\dfrac{6x^2+5x-3}{x(x+1)^2}\) in partial fractions.

Step 1 — Identify the form

Denominator has: \(x\) (distinct), \((x+1)\) (repeated, so needs two terms).

$$\frac{6x^2+5x-3}{x(x+1)^2} \equiv \frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x+1}+\frac{C}{(x+1)^2}$$
Step 2 — Multiply both sides by \(x(x+1)^2\)
$$6x^2+5x-3 \equiv A(x+1)^2 + Bx(x+1) + Cx$$
Step 3 — Substitute roots

Let \(x=0\): \(-3 = A(1) \Rightarrow A = -3\).

Let \(x=-1\): \(6-5-3 = C(-1) \Rightarrow -2 = -C \Rightarrow C = 2\).

Step 4 — Find B by equating coefficients of \(x^2\)

Expand the RHS: \(A(x^2+2x+1)+Bx(x+1)+Cx = (A+B)x^2+\cdots\)

Coefficient of \(x^2\): \(6 = A+B = -3+B \Rightarrow B = 9\).

Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{-3}{x}+\frac{9}{x+1}+\frac{2}{(x+1)^2}}$$
Worked Example 2 — Improper Fraction

Express \(\dfrac{3x^2-2x-1}{(x-1)(x+2)}\) in partial fractions.

Step 1 — Check degree

Top is degree 2. Bottom expands to \(x^2+x-2\), also degree 2. Since degrees are equal, this is improper. Divide first.

Step 2 — Polynomial long division

Divide \(3x^2-2x-1\) by \(x^2+x-2\):

\(3x^2-2x-1 = 3(x^2+x-2) + (-5x+5)\)

Check: \(3(x^2+x-2) = 3x^2+3x-6\). Then \((3x^2-2x-1)-(3x^2+3x-6) = -5x+5\). ✓

$$\frac{3x^2-2x-1}{(x-1)(x+2)} = 3 + \frac{-5x+5}{(x-1)(x+2)}$$
Step 3 — Decompose the remainder

Note \(-5x+5 = -5(x-1)\), so \(\dfrac{-5(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+2)} = \dfrac{-5}{x+2}\).

Alternatively, use the standard method: \(\dfrac{-5x+5}{(x-1)(x+2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\).

\(-5x+5\equiv A(x+2)+B(x-1)\). At \(x=1\): \(0=3A\Rightarrow A=0\). At \(x=-2\): \(15=-3B\Rightarrow B=-5\).

Final Answer
$$\boxed{3 - \frac{5}{x+2}}$$

Question 7

Express \(\dfrac{3x^2}{(x-1)(x+2)}\) in partial fractions (improper).

Full Solutions
Solution 1

\(\dfrac{11x-1}{(x+2)(2x-1)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x+2}+\dfrac{B}{2x-1}\). \(11x-1\equiv A(2x-1)+B(x+2)\).

\(x=-2:\;-23=-5A\Rightarrow A=\dfrac{23}{5}\).\quad \(x=\tfrac{1}{2}:\;\dfrac{9}{2}=\dfrac{5B}{2}\Rightarrow B=\dfrac{9}{5}\).

$$\boxed{\frac{23}{5(x+2)}+\frac{9}{5(2x-1)}}$$
Solution 2

\(\dfrac{5x+3}{(x+1)^2}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{(x+1)^2}\). \(5x+3\equiv A(x+1)+B\).

\(x=-1:\;-2=B\Rightarrow B=-2\). Coefficient of \(x\): \(5=A\Rightarrow A=5\).

$$\boxed{\frac{5}{x+1}-\frac{2}{(x+1)^2}}$$
Solution 3

\(\dfrac{4x^2+3x-2}{x(x+1)(x-2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x}+\dfrac{B}{x+1}+\dfrac{C}{x-2}\).

\(4x^2+3x-2\equiv A(x+1)(x-2)+Bx(x-2)+Cx(x+1)\).

\(x=0:\;-2=A(-2)\Rightarrow A=1.\quad x=-1:\;-1=B(-1)(-3)\Rightarrow B=-\tfrac{1}{3}.\quad x=2:\;16=C(2)(3)\Rightarrow C=\tfrac{8}{3}.\)

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{3(x+1)}+\frac{8}{3(x-2)}}$$
Solution 4

\(\dfrac{2x}{(x-3)(x+3)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-3}+\dfrac{B}{x+3}\). \(2x\equiv A(x+3)+B(x-3)\).

\(x=3:\;6=6A\Rightarrow A=1.\quad x=-3:\;-6=-6B\Rightarrow B=1.\)

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{x-3}+\frac{1}{x+3}}$$
Solution 5

\(\dfrac{7x-5}{(x-1)(x+2)^2}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}+\dfrac{C}{(x+2)^2}\). Multiply: \(7x-5\equiv A(x+2)^2+B(x-1)(x+2)+C(x-1)\).

\(x=1:\;2=9A\Rightarrow A=\tfrac{2}{9}.\quad x=-2:\;-19=-3C\Rightarrow C=\tfrac{19}{3}.\)

Coeff of \(x^2:\;0=A+B\Rightarrow B=-\tfrac{2}{9}.\)

$$\boxed{\frac{2}{9(x-1)}-\frac{2}{9(x+2)}+\frac{19}{3(x+2)^2}}$$
Solution 6

Top degree = bottom degree = 2, so improper. Bottom expands to \(x^2+3x+2\).

\(x^2+3x+5 = 1\cdot(x^2+3x+2)+3\). So quotient = 1, remainder = 3.

\(\dfrac{3}{(x+1)(x+2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\). \(x=-1:\;3=A.\quad x=-2:\;3=-B\Rightarrow B=-3.\)

$$\boxed{1+\frac{3}{x+1}-\frac{3}{x+2}}$$
Solution 7

Top degree 2 = bottom degree 2, improper. Bottom expands to \(x^2+x-2\).

\(3x^2 = 3(x^2+x-2)+(-3x+6)\). Quotient = 3, remainder = \(-3x+6\).

\(\dfrac{-3x+6}{(x-1)(x+2)}\equiv\dfrac{A}{x-1}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\). \(x=1:\;3=3A\Rightarrow A=1.\quad x=-2:\;12=-3B\Rightarrow B=-4.\)

$$\boxed{3+\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{4}{x+2}}$$
Skill 24

Binomial Expansion — Negative & Fractional Indices

What It Is

At GCSE and early A-level you used the binomial theorem for positive integer powers like \((1+x)^5\), giving a finite number of terms. When the power \(n\) is a negative number or a fraction, the expansion gives an infinite series that only converges (gives a finite, useful answer) when \(|x| < 1\).

This is hugely powerful because it lets you expand things like \(\dfrac{1}{(1+x)^2}\) or \(\sqrt{1+x}\) as polynomials, which makes them easier to integrate, differentiate, or approximate.

The formula looks identical to the standard binomial series — the only difference is that \(n\) is no longer a positive integer, and the series never terminates.

The Binomial Series Formula
$$\boxed{(1+x)^n = 1 + nx + \frac{n(n-1)}{2!}x^2 + \frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}x^3 + \cdots}$$

Valid only when \(|x| < 1\).

For \((a+bx)^n\) where \(a\neq 1\): factor out \(a^n\) first.

$$(a+bx)^n = a^n\!\left(1+\frac{bx}{a}\right)^n$$

Then apply the formula with \(x\) replaced by \(\dfrac{bx}{a}\). Valid when \(\left|\dfrac{bx}{a}\right| < 1\), i.e., \(|x| < \dfrac{a}{b}\).

Where to Apply It

Use this when the question asks you to expand an expression like \((1-2x)^{-1}\), \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+x}}\), or \(\dfrac{3}{(1+x)^2}\) as a series in ascending powers of \(x\). It is also used after partial fraction decomposition to expand each term separately (Skill 23 → Skill 24).

Worked Example 1

Expand \((1-2x)^{-2}\) up to and including the term in \(x^3\). State the range of validity.

Step 1 — Identify n and the substitution

Here \(n = -2\) and we replace \(x\) with \(-2x\) in the formula.

Step 2 — Write out the formula terms
$$\text{Term 0: }1$$ $$\text{Term 1: }n(-2x) = (-2)(-2x) = 4x$$ $$\text{Term 2: }\frac{n(n-1)}{2!}(-2x)^2 = \frac{(-2)(-3)}{2}(4x^2) = 12x^2$$ $$\text{Term 3: }\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}{3!}(-2x)^3 = \frac{(-2)(-3)(-4)}{6}(-8x^3) = 32x^3$$
Step 3 — State the expansion and validity
$$\text{Valid when }|-2x|<1\Rightarrow|x|<\tfrac{1}{2}$$
Final Answer
$$\boxed{(1-2x)^{-2} \approx 1 + 4x + 12x^2 + 32x^3 + \cdots,\quad |x|<\tfrac{1}{2}}$$
Worked Example 2

Expand \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{4+x}}\) up to and including the term in \(x^2\). State the range of validity.

Step 1 — Rewrite in index form and factor out
$$(4+x)^{-1/2} = \left(4\!\left(1+\frac{x}{4}\right)\right)^{-1/2} = 4^{-1/2}\!\left(1+\frac{x}{4}\right)^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(1+\frac{x}{4}\right)^{-1/2}$$
Step 2 — Apply binomial formula with \(n=-\tfrac{1}{2}\), substitute \(\tfrac{x}{4}\) for \(x\)
$$\text{Term 1: }1$$ $$\text{Term 2: }\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\!\left(\tfrac{x}{4}\right) = -\frac{x}{8}$$ $$\text{Term 3: }\frac{(-\frac{1}{2})(-\frac{3}{2})}{2!}\!\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{\frac{3}{4}}{2}\cdot\frac{x^2}{16} = \frac{3x^2}{128}$$
Step 3 — Multiply through by \(\tfrac{1}{2}\)
$$\frac{1}{2}\!\left(1 - \frac{x}{8} + \frac{3x^2}{128}+\cdots\right) = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{x}{16} + \frac{3x^2}{256}+\cdots$$
Step 4 — State validity

Valid when \(\left|\dfrac{x}{4}\right|<1\), i.e., \(|x|<4\).

Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{1}{\sqrt{4+x}} \approx \frac{1}{2} - \frac{x}{16} + \frac{3x^2}{256} + \cdots,\quad |x|<4}$$

Skill 25

Algebraic Long Division

What It Is

Algebraic long division is the polynomial equivalent of numerical long division. It allows you to divide one polynomial by another to get a quotient (the result) and a remainder. When the remainder is zero, the divisor is a factor of the dividend.

The process works by repeatedly asking: "what do I multiply the leading term of the divisor by to match the leading term of the current expression?" You then subtract and repeat until the remaining degree is less than the divisor's degree.

This is essential for converting improper algebraic fractions into mixed form (polynomial + proper fraction) before applying partial fractions or integration. It is also used to verify polynomial factors and to perform polynomial division in proof questions.

The Division Algorithm

Any polynomial division can be written as:

$$f(x) = d(x)\cdot q(x) + r(x)$$

where \(f(x)\) is the dividend, \(d(x)\) is the divisor, \(q(x)\) is the quotient, and \(r(x)\) is the remainder.

The degree of \(r(x)\) must be strictly less than the degree of \(d(x)\).

As a fraction:

$$\frac{f(x)}{d(x)} = q(x) + \frac{r(x)}{d(x)}$$

The Remainder Theorem (useful check): When \(f(x)\) is divided by \((x-a)\), the remainder equals \(f(a)\).

Where to Apply It

Use algebraic long division whenever you have an improper algebraic fraction (numerator degree ≥ denominator degree) and need to convert it before doing partial fractions, integration, or binomial expansion. Also used when a question asks you to divide polynomials or when factorising a cubic after finding one factor by inspection or the factor theorem.

Worked Example 1

Divide \(2x^3 - 3x^2 + x + 5\) by \((x-2)\).

Step 1 — Set up the division

Divide the leading term of the dividend by the leading term of the divisor:

$$2x^3 \div x = 2x^2$$

Multiply \(2x^2\) by \((x-2)\): \(2x^3 - 4x^2\). Subtract from dividend:

$$(2x^3-3x^2+x+5)-(2x^3-4x^2) = x^2+x+5$$
Step 2 — Repeat with the new expression

\(x^2 \div x = x\). Multiply \(x\) by \((x-2)\): \(x^2-2x\). Subtract:

$$(x^2+x+5)-(x^2-2x) = 3x+5$$
Step 3 — Repeat again

\(3x \div x = 3\). Multiply \(3\) by \((x-2)\): \(3x-6\). Subtract:

$$(3x+5)-(3x-6) = 11$$

Degree of 11 (degree 0) is less than degree of \((x-2)\) (degree 1). Stop. Remainder = 11.

Step 4 — Check using remainder theorem

\(f(2) = 2(8)-3(4)+2+5 = 16-12+2+5 = 11\). ✓

Final Answer
$$\boxed{2x^3-3x^2+x+5 = (x-2)(2x^2+x+3)+11}$$ $$\text{or equivalently: }\frac{2x^3-3x^2+x+5}{x-2} = 2x^2+x+3+\frac{11}{x-2}$$
Worked Example 2

Express \(\dfrac{x^3+2x^2-3}{x^2+x-2}\) in the form \(q(x)+\dfrac{r(x)}{x^2+x-2}\).

Step 1 — Identify this is improper

Degree of top = 3. Degree of bottom = 2. Since 3 > 2, we must divide.

Step 2 — First division step

\(x^3 \div x^2 = x\). Multiply \(x\) by \((x^2+x-2)\): \(x^3+x^2-2x\). Subtract:

$$(x^3+2x^2-3)-(x^3+x^2-2x) = x^2+2x-3$$
Step 3 — Second division step

\(x^2 \div x^2 = 1\). Multiply \(1\) by \((x^2+x-2)\): \(x^2+x-2\). Subtract:

$$(x^2+2x-3)-(x^2+x-2) = x-1$$

Degree of \(x-1\) (degree 1) is less than degree of \(x^2+x-2\) (degree 2). Stop. Quotient = \(x+1\), remainder = \(x-1\).

Step 4 — Check

\((x^2+x-2)(x+1)+(x-1) = x^3+x^2-2x+x^2+x-2+x-1 = x^3+2x^2-3\). ✓

Final Answer
$$\boxed{\frac{x^3+2x^2-3}{x^2+x-2} = (x+1)+\frac{x-1}{x^2+x-2}}$$

Full Solutions
Solution 1

Divide \(x^3+3x^2-x-3\) by \((x+1)\):

\(x^3\div x=x^2\). Mult: \(x^3+x^2\). Subtract: \(2x^2-x-3\).

\(2x^2\div x=2x\). Mult: \(2x^2+2x\). Subtract: \(-3x-3\).

\(-3x\div x=-3\). Mult: \(-3x-3\). Subtract: 0. Remainder = 0.

$$\boxed{x^3+3x^2-x-3 = (x+1)(x^2+2x-3)}$$

Check: \((x+1)(x^2+2x-3)=(x+1)(x+3)(x-1)\). Fully factorised: \((x+1)(x+3)(x-1)\).

Solution 2

\(2x^3\div x=2x^2\). Mult: \(2x^3-2x^2\). Subtract: \(3x^2-5x+2\).

\(3x^2\div x=3x\). Mult: \(3x^2-3x\). Subtract: \(-2x+2\).

\(-2x\div x=-2\). Mult: \(-2x+2\). Subtract: 0.

$$\boxed{2x^3+x^2-5x+2=(x-1)(2x^2+3x-2)}$$

Check via remainder theorem: \(f(1)=2+1-5+2=0\). ✓

Solution 3

Write: \(x^3+0x^2+0x-8\) divided by \((x-2)\).

\(x^3\div x=x^2\). Mult: \(x^3-2x^2\). Subtract: \(2x^2+0x-8\).

\(2x^2\div x=2x\). Mult: \(2x^2-4x\). Subtract: \(4x-8\).

\(4x\div x=4\). Mult: \(4x-8\). Subtract: 0.

$$\boxed{x^3-8=(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}$$

This is the standard difference of cubes factorisation. ✓

Solution 4

Divide \(3x^2+5x-1\) by \((x+2)\):

\(3x^2\div x=3x\). Mult: \(3x^2+6x\). Subtract: \(-x-1\).

\(-x\div x=-1\). Mult: \(-x-2\). Subtract: \(1\). Remainder = 1.

$$\boxed{\frac{3x^2+5x-1}{x+2} = 3x-1+\frac{1}{x+2}}$$

Solution 5

Divide \(x^3-x^2+x-1\) by \((x^2+1)\):

\(x^3\div x^2=x\). Mult: \(x^3+x\). Subtract: \(-x^2+0\cdot x-1\).

$$\boxed{\frac{x^3-x^2+x-1}{x^2+1} = x-1}$$

Zero remainder confirms \((x^2+1)\) divides exactly. The cubic \(x^3-x^2+x-1=(x^2+1)(x-1)\).

Divide \(4x^3-6x^2+2x+3\) by \((2x-3)\):

\(4x^3\div 2x=2x^2\). Mult: \(4x^3-6x^2\). Subtract: \(0\cdot x^2+2x+3\).

\(0\cdot x^2\div 2x=0\). So bring down: remainder is \(2x+3\).

\(2x\div 2x=1\). Mult: \(2x-3\). Subtract: \(6\). Remainder = 6.

$$\boxed{4x^3-6x^2+2x+3=(2x-3)(2x^2+1)+6}$$ $$\text{i.e., }\frac{4x^3-6x^2+2x+3}{2x-3}=2x^2+1+\frac{6}{2x-3}$$

Check via remainder theorem: \(f(\tfrac{3}{2})=4\cdot\tfrac{27}{8}-6\cdot\tfrac{9}{4}+3+3=\tfrac{27}{2}-\tfrac{27}{2}+6=6\). ✓

Solution 7

Check \((x-3)\) is a factor: \(f(3)=27-54+33-6=0\). ✓ So remainder = 0, hence \((x-3)\) is a factor.

Divide \(x^3-6x^2+11x-6\) by \((x-3)\):

\(x^3\div x=x^2\). Mult: \(x^3-3x^2\). Subtract: \(-3x^2+11x-6\).

\(-3x^2\div x=-3x\). Mult: \(-3x^2+9x\). Subtract: \(2x-6\).

\(2x\div x=2\). Mult: \(2x-6\). Subtract: 0.

So \(x^3-6x^2+11x-6=(x-3)(x^2-3x+2)=(x-3)(x-1)(x-2)\).

$$\boxed{x^3-6x^2+11x-6=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)}$$
